## TL;DR

Scientists at Germany's University of Hohenheim propose that very large solar farms in coastal deserts could heat air enough to trigger convection and rainfall, with simulations suggesting a 50-by-50 km pair could water 125,000 extra people per year in the United Arab Emirates. The United Arab Emirates awarded up to $1.5 million to test the idea in the Arabian Peninsula. The video argues the physics may work but the required scale, cost, and global side effects make deployment questionable. <!-- block:ba35f40a-f845-45a8-ab41-34382199cef2 -->

## Key ideas

- **Waste-heat convection mechanism:** Solar panels waste most sunlight as heat, creating rising warm-air currents that can drag moist sea-breeze air upward to form clouds and rain. Branch says solar parks darken the land surface and increase absorbed radiation, but only coastal deserts with incoming moisture can work. [0:00](https://youtu.be/kcQ7n9-hkRU?si=hw67wvtOU3J-1uUr&t=0) <!-- block:7bfa39e1-b44e-4449-9642-639dcac27347 -->
  <!-- aliases: solar farm rainmaking; branch dark-surface heating; hohenheim convection theory -->
- **Simulated scale threshold:** The University of Hohenheim team of Oliver Branch and Volker Wulfmeyer simulated artificial black surfaces and found a 10-by-10 km pair did little, while a 20-by-20 km pair could supply water for 31,000 people per year in the United Arab Emirates. Artificial black surfaces absorb more sunlight than real solar panels, so real solar farms would perform worse. [0:00](https://youtu.be/kcQ7n9-hkRU?si=hw67wvtOU3J-1uUr&t=0) <!-- block:7dbb7d14-6600-4081-9e89-1789d584212b -->
  <!-- aliases: hohenheim rainfall simulation; branch 20-by-20 rain threshold; uae solar farm size -->
- **Coastal placement constraint:** Branch is focused on coastal deserts because rain only comes if heating meets moisture-carrying sea-breeze air. Global Energy Monitor counts over 81,000 utility solar phases, but only Hobq and Talatan approach 400 square km and neither sits in a coastal desert. That spending would consume a large share of the $450 billion invested in solar in 2025, with no rain-specific funding at that scale.
