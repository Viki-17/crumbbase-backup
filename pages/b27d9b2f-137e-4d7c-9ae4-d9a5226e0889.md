## TL;DR

Money is not a thing but a shared agreement to measure value, from Yap stones to Mesopotamian clay debt records. Paper receipts, goldsmith notes, and modern bank loans all worked until issuers quietly shortened the ruler by printing more. Because central banks target 2% inflation, savers lose while governments as the biggest borrowers win. <!-- block:e5151c00-b1a8-4fa9-be9a-5586d35a9ae3 -->

## Key ideas

- **Money as agreement:** The people of Yap use enormous stone discs as money. A sunken stone no one can see still circulates because the island agrees it belongs to one family. <!-- block:ffe534db-986d-43a6-9d34-eba342c092e3 -->
  <!-- aliases: yap island stones; yap money agreement; stone money -->
- **Barter never existed:** Adam Smith invented the barter story in 1776 as logic, not history. Caroline Humphrey concluded barter came after money as an emergency backup when currencies collapsed in Russia in the 1990s and Argentina in 2002. <!-- block:ba64ce33-4b34-4e68-844a-d1577c3cb7c1 -->
  <!-- aliases: adam smith barter myth; caroline humphrey barter; barter economy debunked -->
- **Debt came first:** The oldest writing ever discovered is a debt record from over 5,000 years ago found in Mesopotamia. The first coins showed up 2,700 years later in Lydia after 2,000 years of recorded promises. <!-- block:e3514a74-9915-407f-be6b-3176503ecff1 -->
  <!-- aliases: mesopotamia debt clay tablets; lydia first coins; credit before coin -->
- **Money as ruler:** Money was supposed to work like a measuring tool, like a ruler. A minute is 60 seconds everywhere, but money is a human agreement that can be changed silently. <!-- block:3dd093be-ec6d-43ef-b417-c048e20dce35 -->
  <!-- aliases: money measuring tool; ruler theory of money; stable measure of value -->
- **Paper money trap:** Merchants in Sichuan during the Song Dynasty left heavy iron with shops and traded receipts instead of metal. The government made it official, creating government-issued paper money called Jiaozi, then printed more for war until trust collapsed. <!-- block:03458e31-7c79-45c1-8d4a-265bd91ecc23 -->
  <!-- aliases: song dynasty sichuan paper money; jiaozi inflation; sichuan iron money receipts -->
- **Banks create money by lending:** Your signature is what created that money. When you sign a $10,000 loan, the bank types $10,000 into your account and collects real interest on numbers it just typed. <!-- block:4c03f8f2-613c-4152-af73-0cebd24eb4b5 -->
  <!-- aliases: bank loan money creation; goldsmith fractional reserve; how loans create money -->
- **Goldsmith fractional reserve:** London goldsmiths stored gold and issued receipts that people traded instead of metal. The goldsmiths lent idle gold secretly, keeping the interest while depositors carried the risk. <!-- block:b31e12fb-3e05-457d-aeb6-8024ffff06e7 -->
  <!-- aliases: london goldsmith receipts; goldsmith fractional reserve; vault receipts trading -->

## Notable claims

- No example of a barter economy has ever been described. (Anthropologist Caroline Humphrey searched across cultures and eras and found zero cases; Adam Smith's 1776 barter story was logic, not history.) <!-- block:918b0644-d92f-438d-80d5-99b641c87059 -->
  <!-- aliases: caroline humphrey barter; adam smith barter myth -->
- China invented paper money first and was destroyed by it first. (Song Dynasty Sichuan shops issued iron receipts, the state issued Jiaozi as one official note, then overprinted for war until people returned to heavy iron coins.) <!-- block:1aaa31c0-25dc-4d78-aa62-8d2f5f265d1a -->
  <!-- aliases: song dynasty paper money; jiaozi collapse -->
- Banks create money when you sign a loan and interest forces endless new debt. (You borrow $10,000 but owe $11,000 that was never created, so someone else must borrow for you to find the extra $1,000, requiring constant new debt forever.) <!-- block:ce6d0ac5-6d2a-4d31-91cb-62169ff19197 -->
  <!-- aliases: bank loan money creation; bank interest debt trap -->
- A 2% inflation target halves savings every 35 years to benefit governments as the biggest borrowers. (Every major central bank targets 2% inflation as price stability; borrowers repay with weaker currency, so state debt is inflated away while savers pay.) <!-- block:c0c0e5b5-4ab3-4d92-8bfb-e5a5472b3779 -->
  <!-- aliases: central bank 2 percent inflation; government borrower inflation benefit -->
